# Changelog

We document notable changes to the data in this file; it's in reverse chronological order (recent changes up top).

## [Unreleased]
### Changed
* Works: versions of one item that the publisher declares in Crossref are now one work. A publisher that registers a DOI for each version (eLife's Reviewed Preprints, its versionless DOI and its version of record; F1000Research, Research Square, Preprints.org, OSF preprints, Cochrane review updates and others) links them with `is-version-of` / `has-version`, `is-same-as` or a Crossmark `new_version` update, and each version became its own work, splitting citations. Two DOIs of one prefix declared versions of one item, of the same type (or a preprint and its published form), now merge into the most-cited one, and the other ids redirect to it. The same goes for one journal's two language editions of an article, which the journal links with `is-translation-of` / `has-translation` (SciELO journals register a DOI per language): a translation published in another journal stays its own work, linked to its original. About 119,000 works merge into about 92,000. Links across two publishers' prefixes, and versions whose records are also linked another way (a review, a notice, a supplement), stay separate. A new version joins its work the night after it is registered. (oxjob #1696)
* Works: the `publication_date` of a work dated by its Crossref record is now the earliest publication date the publisher states in that record (issued, published online, published in print, posted or approved). The day the DOI was registered with Crossref, and the day of its latest deposit, count only when the record states no online or posted date: then they stand in for when the work went online, as for publishers that deposit only a print date. Before, the registration day always counted, so a DOI registered ahead of its stated online date was dated by its registration: 10.1162/rest_a_01152, registered 22 December 2021 and published online and in print in March 2024, read 2021 and now reads 2024. About 9.2M Crossref records change date, every one to a later date; about 832,000 change year, and `publication_year` follows. A date more than a year ahead of today is still replaced by the day the work first appeared. (oxjob #1157)
* Works: `related_works` is recomputed for every work: up to 10 works closest in meaning to each one (the nearest neighbours of its title, abstract and venue in OpenAlex's Qwen3 embeddings), never paratext, errata, retractions, reviews or retracted works, and never a work with the same title. 437.5M works have a list (95.8% of titled core works, 92.8% of titled expansion-pack works; before: 64.9% of core works and no expansion-pack work, a one-time 2025 copy of an older topic-based method). Blind-judged on random works, the new lists' items average 2.34 on a 0-3 relatedness scale vs 0.72 for the old lists. New works now get their list the day after they are added (a nightly step; recall of the 10 nearest vs exact search 0.93). The backfill did not move `updated_date` (snapshot users need a full reload of works); a new work's list landing does. (oxjob #1648)
* Works: `topics` and `primary_topic` now come from a new topic classifier (a Qwen3-8B model trained on 2M works labelled by Claude Opus 5.5, reading the title, abstract and venue). The classifier swap itself did not change the topics: same 4,516 topics, names, IDs and subfield / field / domain hierarchy. Some names and parents changed later, in November 2026 (next entry). Each work gets up to 3 topics; `score` is the model's calibrated probability that the topic is the work's main one, so 2nd and 3rd scores are often small. Works the model cannot classify (mostly records with no abstract and little title text) get no topic. On a fresh test set of random works the primary topic is right 81.7% of the time vs 24.8% for the previous model; about 447M works change topics. `fwci`, `citation_normalized_percentile` and `cited_by_percentile_year` move with the new subfields (their cohort). `updated_date` does not move for this change: snapshot users need a full reload. Code, weights and evaluations: https://github.com/ourresearch/openalex-topic-classification/tree/main/v2 (oxjob #1531)
* Topics: 1,072 of the 4,516 topics changed name or parent in November 2026. Topic IDs did not change, and no topic was added, removed, split or merged. 3 Nov: 22 topics whose names collided with another topic's were renamed (T10032 "Marine and coastal ecosystems" is now "Marine Plankton and Biogeochemistry"), 7 subfields got back their ASJC labels (2716 "Genetics" is now "Genetics (clinical)") and 7 subfield descriptions that came from wrong Wikidata matches were rewritten; works carried the new names from 4 Nov (oxjob #1565). 6 Nov: an audit of every topic's works moved 972 topics to another subfield (668 of them to another field, 276 to another domain), and 34 topics whose names used another topic's words in a different order were renamed; subfields 1502 Bioengineering and 3614 Radiological and Ultrasound Technology now hold no topics but stay in the hierarchy (oxjob #1565). 8 Nov: T10019 moved from 1402 Accounting to 2003 Finance and T11381 from 2746 Surgery to 2725 Infectious Diseases (oxjob #1565). 23-24 Nov: 27 more topics moved to another subfield and 31 topics with near-synonym names were renamed (oxjob #2297). Works' `topics` and `primary_topic` carry the new names and parents, and `fwci` and `citation_normalized_percentile` moved with the subfields (their cohort). `updated_date` moved for these works except for the 6 Nov changes: to pick those up, snapshot users need a full reload of works. Every topic's before and after, by date: https://help.openalex.org/data/topic-hierarchy-changes/ (oxjob #2335)
* Works: `sustainable_development_goals` now comes from a new classifier: a per-goal head on the work's Qwen3 embedding, trained on 200K Jev (TypeSafe) judgments of the 17 goals and calibrated per goal. Same element shape (`id`, `display_name`, `score`); a goal is listed when its `score` (the classifier's confidence, 0-1, higher = surer) is at least 0.4, highest first. Works without an embedding have an empty list. Display names follow the UN wording: "Quality education", "Life on land", "Peace, justice, and strong institutions". Against a judge committee on 2,000 random works the new tags score F1 0.68 vs 0.29 for the previous model, and better on all 17 goals. (oxjob #1300)
* Works: about 2.58M works that showed no page numbers now carry the first and last page their repository records cite. DOAJ, Pure and other repositories cite an article as "Ho Chi Minh City Open University Journal of Science - Economics and Business Administration, Vol 6, Iss 1, Pp 32-48 (2016)" or "..., pp. 61-72" and have no page fields of their own, so works known from such records (about 1.57M of them without a DOI) showed `biblio.first_page` and `biblio.last_page` as null (W3092536694 now shows 32-48). A citation is read only when it states exactly one page range, and only when all the work's repository citations agree on it. A range that starts at page 1 is not read: DOAJ cites an article in a journal that numbers articles rather than pages by its PDF's page span ("BMC Psychiatry, Vol 20, Iss 1, Pp 1-9" for article 61). The range only fills empty pages: a page another record, a Crossref article number or a curation gives always wins. Where a work already had a first page, the cited one agreed 98.6% of the time. `updated_date` moved for these works. (oxjob #1098)
### Fixed
* Authorships: an affiliation string that names only "Korea University" (no city) is no longer linked to both Korea University in Seoul and Korea University in Kodaira, Tokyo, and one that names only "Western University" no longer links Western University in Cambodia or Thailand along with Western University in London, Ontario. The affiliation matcher kept both namesakes on such strings, so new papers from Seoul also showed the Tokyo school (about 400 new strings a day in November 2026). For these listed same-name pairs the smaller namesake now stays only when the string carries its own location (Kodaira, Tokyo, Japan, 朝鮮大学校; Phnom Penh, Cambodia; Thailand, Bangkok, Pathum Thani and so on); a string with such a word keeps both. Applies to strings the matcher answers from now on; strings answered before were corrected by curations. (oxjob #1803)
* Works: about 990 Science research articles (2013-2021) that showed the journal's editor's summary as their `abstract` ('Skyrmions in a frustrated magnet Skyrmions—tiny, topologically protected whirlpools of spin ... Science, this issue p. 914') now show the authors' abstract. Science deposits only the editor's summary with Crossref for these articles; the abstract is in the article's PubMed record, but our PubMed reader often kept only the end of it (the text after the last subscript, superscript or italic), so the longer summary won. A work that would show the summary now shows the authors' text from another record when one holds it: the full PubMed abstract, or a PubMed Central, repository or preprint copy. About 200 works with no such record keep the summary.

* Works: about 103,000 more supplementary files deposited on Figshare as "Additional file ..." are now parts of their article (`parent_works`, relationship `component`) and typed `supplementary-materials`, so they leave default lists and search like the other supplements. Titles with a label before the article's title ("Additional file 1: Figure S1. of <title>", about 59,000, most of them typed `article` until now) or with no file number ("Additional file of <title>") were not recognised, and a file whose article title is carried by two works, or whose article was later retitled "RETRACTED ARTICLE: ...", stayed unlinked or went to a preprint copy; such a file now takes the article its DataCite record names (IsSupplementTo). Also, an AACR supplement no longer stays unlinked because its article has a correction with the same title (e.g. the figures of W2152260155).
* Works: about 15,900 items in Spanish-language journals that were typed `article` now show their form. About 10,900 book reviews, titled as the citation of the book they review, are now typed `book-review`: a title with an ISBN in it, or one that ends with the book's page count ('Ben Goldacre: Mala ciencia. Paidós. Barcelona, 2011, 400 pp.', W103790632). About 5,000 journal items titled only 'Introducción', 'Prólogo', 'Prefacio', 'Presentación' or 'Nota editorial', an issue's opening note, are now typed `editorial`. In repository records a bare 'Prólogo' is often a book's prologue, so those keep their type, except 'Nota editorial'. A type curation still wins. (oxjob #1160)
* Authorships: an author no longer keeps an institution that came only from a library's cataloguing notice or a copyright line we stopped reading as an affiliation. * Works: about 10,700 copies in KAUST Repository, King Abdullah University of Science and Technology's repository, that read closed now read open, with their PDF as `pdf_url`. * Works: an item on an institution's Figshare portal now shows the institution's own copy as its `primary_location`. Universities and other organisations (Deakin, Monash, Swinburne, La Trobe, Carnegie Mellon, ICES and hundreds of others) run their repositories on Figshare, and each item reaches us twice: as figshare.com's own record, filed under the general Figshare source, and as the institution's DataCite DOI, filed under the institution's repository. The figshare.com record states a version and the DOI record none, so it ranked first, and about 335,000 works (35,000 in the default corpus) listed Figshare instead of the institution, e.g. W6889719546 (10.26187/deakin.28693058) now leads with Deakin Research Online. Both records are matched by the Figshare article number in the DOI record's link. Only `primary_location` and its place in `locations` change: the work's type, open-access status and best open copy are unchanged.
* Authorships: a copyright line is no longer listed as an author's affiliation when the author has another affiliation string. Journal pages and PDFs carry the journal's or publisher's notice ('Copyright© Faculty of Engineering, University of Nigeria, Nsukka', '©American Society for Engineering Education, 2023', '©1992 Brown University' on Quarterly of Applied Mathematics papers), and we read it as an affiliation of every author and linked it to the copyright holder. * Authorships: a work no longer names an institution that was merged into another. When an institution record is merged (its `merge_into_id` set), the step that builds each work's authorships kept the old id wherever an affiliation string was still matched to it, so `authorships.institutions` and `authorships.affiliations.institution_ids` could carry an id with no institution of its own (I4210133110 on W107893752, for Tampere University), and new works kept picking such ids up. An id merged into 'Deleted Institution' (I4389424196) is dropped. On 21 November 2026, 319 works carried 81 such ids; other works are unchanged. `updated_date` moved for these works. (oxjob #322)
* Works: AACR's supplementary files are now parts of their article, not separate works. The American Association for Cancer Research puts each article's figures, tables, supplementary files and its "Data from <title>" collection on its Figshare portal, each with its own Crossref DOI (10.1158/<ISSN>.<number>; collections 10.1158/<ISSN>.c.<number>) and a title such as "Supplementary Figure 2 from <article title>", so one article could show up 15 times in a title search (W7214772182 and its 14 files). About 270,700 such files on about 32,000 articles now list their article in `parent_works` (relationship `component`), the article counts them in `child_works_count`, and like other components they are hidden by default; all of them are typed `supplementary-materials` (about 111,000 change type, mostly from `other`). A file is linked only when exactly one traditional work has the title after its first " from " (or, of several, the only one in the same AACR journal). `updated_date` moved for these works. (oxjob #2179)
* Works: a repository copy's `version` is the one its own repository record declares. Where a record's `dc:type` states a version (`info:eu-repo/semantics/publishedVersion`, a COAR version URI, or a bare `publishedVersion`, `acceptedVersion`, `VoR` or `AM`), that version now outranks the item's web page and PDF, which we read as `submittedVersion` unless they say otherwise. About 11.8M repository copies on 10.9M works showed a version their own record contradicted, 10.1M of them a published version shown as `submittedVersion` (DORA, Durham Research Online and most DSpace repositories among them). A record listing several versions counts as the earliest. A record that types itself a preprint stays `submittedVersion` even when it also says `publishedVersion`, which engrXiv, DESY, RWTH Aachen and some other repositories use to mean "published on this server" (about 17,000 works). Records we hold only from the pre-2025 backfill harvests are not covered yet. (oxjob #1132)
* Authorships: members of a collaboration group credited on a paper ("... and the CleanHand4 collaboration group") no longer all show the lead authors' institution. * Works: a work held in two records of the same rank (most often a JSTOR copy, DOI `10.2307/...`, beside the publisher's own DOI, or an alias DOI) no longer mixes their page numbers in `biblio`. * Authorships: an author's `raw_orcid` no longer comes from Microsoft Academic Graph (MAG).
