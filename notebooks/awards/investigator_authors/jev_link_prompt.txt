You are matching one investigator named on one research grant to author profiles in a scholarly
database. Each candidate profile is summarised from that profile's own papers. Profiles are often
split: one real person can have several profiles.

Question: which candidate, if any, is the same real person as the investigator?

1. SAME PERSON means the investigator and the candidate's author are one human being. If two
   candidates look like the same person, either is right: pick the one with more matching evidence
   and set "split": true.

2. NAME MUST BE COMPATIBLE (needed for any match):
   a. Family names are equal after ignoring case, accents, spaces, hyphens and apostrophes
      (Müller = Mueller = Muller; García-López = Garcia Lopez).
   b. Given names are equal, or one is the initial of the other (J. = John). Two different full
      given names are not compatible (Ken is not Kenji; John is not James). A different middle
      initial is a warning, not a veto.
   c. Order does not matter: "Wang Wei", "Wei Wang" and "WANG, Wei" are one name. If the grant's
      given and family fields look swapped, try both orders.
   d. A name in another script is compatible with its standard romanisations (王伟 = Wang Wei;
      佐藤 健 = Sato Ken; Иванов = Ivanov). Many people share one romanisation, so this only makes
      the name compatible. It is not evidence.
   e. A different family name (marriage or change) is compatible only through 3A.

3. MATCH only when the name is compatible AND one of these holds:
   A. ORCID: the grant gives an ORCID, the candidate's profile has the same ORCID, and nothing
      in rule 4 contradicts it.
   B. FUNDED PAPER: the candidate is an author on a paper this grant funded, and no other
      name-compatible author is on those papers.
   C. PLACE AND FIELD, both:
      (i) the candidate's papers show the grant's institution (or a hospital or institute that
          belongs to it) at some point from 3 years before the grant starts to 5 years after; and
      (ii) the candidate's papers fit the grant's subject.

4. NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE: answer "unsure". This is the default.
   - The name alone matches, however rare it is.
   - Name and field match but there is no place; or name and place match but the field does not
     fit or cannot be told.
   - Two or more candidates meet 3C, or 3A and 3B point to different candidates, and the cards do
     not show they are one person. Common names (Wang, Kim, Smith, Sato) often land here. That is
     fine.
   - The evidence conflicts, for example the ORCID points to a candidate in an unrelated field
     who was never near the grant's institution.

5. NOT THIS PERSON: answer "none" when no candidate has a compatible name, or when every compatible
   candidate fails on evidence against, with no 3A or 3B: a different field, publishing only 10+
   years before or after the grant, or never near the grant's institution or country.

6. MOVES: people change jobs. A candidate who was at the grant's institution inside the window
   counts even if they are elsewhere now. Being elsewhere at grant time is not evidence against a
   match when 3A or 3B holds.

7. PRECISION OVER RECALL: a wrong person is worse than a missing one. When in doubt, "unsure".

        ORCID if the profile has one; whether they are on a paper this grant funded}
